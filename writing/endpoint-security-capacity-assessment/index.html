From a Slow Phone to a GRC Risk Assessment: My First Learning Project

I treated a slow Android phone like a mini GRC assessment: evidence, 8 findings, a 5x5 risk register and ISO 27001 mapping. Here is what I learned.

By Prathamesh Chavan14 November 20268 min read

  • GRC
  • Risk assessment
  • ISO 27001
  • Android security
  • Learning project

Key takeaways

  • The OTG device was not the cause. A full phone with almost no free RAM was.
  • Eight findings, three rated High, scored on a 5x5 matrix and mapped to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A.
  • Evidence beat assumptions: my first guess was wrong.

The problem

My Android phone became very slow right after I connected a USB OTG device. The obvious explanation was the OTG device. Instead of just fixing it, I treated the slowdown as an availability incident and investigated it the way a GRC analyst would: collect evidence, record findings, score the risk, recommend controls and report what risk remains.

This is a self-study project to practise cybersecurity GRC. It is not an audit of any organisation, and it uses my own personal device.

Collecting evidence

I used Android Debug Bridge (ADB) from a Windows PC to read the phone's state. Each command produces output that can be quoted as evidence for a finding.

CommandWhat it tells you
adb shell top -n 1 -m 10CPU load, top processes, memory totals
adb shell dumpsys batteryBattery level, health and temperature
adb shell dumpsys meminfoMemory use per process
adb shell df -hHow full each partition is
adb shell dumpsys diskstatsStorage by category and by app

What the evidence ruled out

  • CPU load: 740% idle out of 800%, so no runaway process.
  • Heat: battery temperature of 25.0 °C, so no thermal throttling.
  • Battery: health reported good, level 63%.

Ruling things out matters as much as finding things. It stops a report from blaming the wrong cause.

The findings

Each finding has an ID, supporting evidence and a severity. Storage and memory were the clear problems, and the Android version turned out to be a separate security issue.

F2Memory exhaustion29 MB of 1.8 GB RAM free; 752 MB of 1.5 GB swap in useHigh
F3Device not restarted for about 7.5 daysUptime counter from dumpsys meminfoLow
F4USB debugging enabled and ADB authorisedSuccessful ADB sessionMedium
F5Unnecessary or unmanaged applicationsGames, OEM engineering and test apps, YouTube holding 3.5 GBMedium
F6Removable media (OTG) used without controlsReported OTG connectionMedium
F7Data recovery relies on user-managed backupsGoogle Photos and a vendor cloud were the only recovery paths foundMedium
F8Unsupported operating systemAndroid 8.1.0, a version that no longer receives Google security patchesHigh

Root cause

Performance: the phone has very little RAM. When memory ran out, Android moved data into swap, which sits on internal storage that was already 97% full. Every app switch and reload became slow, and the indexing that starts when a USB drive is connected added load to a system with no headroom. The OTG device contributed, but it was not the root cause.

Security: the device runs an end-of-support Android version, so newly discovered vulnerabilities will not be fixed. That is independent of the slowdown.

Scoring the risk

I scored each risk from 1 to 5 for likelihood and impact. The rating is likelihood multiplied by impact: 1 to 5 is Low, 6 to 12 is Medium and 13 to 25 is High. The scores are my own judgement and would normally be validated by the risk owner.

Remediation plan

Actions are labelled by control type: corrective controls fix an existing problem, preventive controls stop one happening, and detective controls find or confirm one.

RefActionTypePriorityFixes
R1Free at least 20% of internal storage by moving media to the SD card or cloudCorrectiveImmediateF1, F2
R2Turn off USB debugging after diagnostics and revoke authorised computersPreventiveImmediateF4
R3Keep banking and work accounts off the device until it is replacedPreventiveImmediateF8
R4Clear app caches and remove unused appsCorrectiveShort termF1, F5
R5Check backups are complete before deleting anythingPreventiveShort termF7

R7Restart regularly and review storage monthlyPreventiveOngoingF2, F3
R8Scan removable media and limit OTG to known devicesPreventiveOngoingF6

Residual risk

After R1 to R8, the performance and configuration risks fall to Low. Two risks remain because they cannot be fixed on this device: the end-of-support operating system and the 1.8 GB RAM limit. I rate the residual risk as Medium. Replacing the device is the only full fix.

What I learned

  1. Evidence beats assumptions. I blamed the OTG device first. The data pointed elsewhere.
  2. A finding needs structure. An ID, evidence, a severity and an owner turn an observation into something someone can act on.
  3. Residual risk is part of the answer. Fixing what you can and reporting what remains is more honest than saying everything is solved.
  4. State your assumptions. Writing down what I had not verified made the report easier to trust.

Limitations

  • The Android version was taken from the device settings and the security patch level was not recorded.
  • The end-of-support status of Android 8.1 should be rechecked against the current Android Security Bulletins.
  • Ownership, encryption status and MDM enrolment were not assessed.
  • The type and file system of the OTG device were not examined.
  • Application names were matched to sizes by position in a package list, so individual names may be off by one.
  • Likelihood and impact scores are judgement calls.

A natural next step is to repeat the ADB checks after cleaning up and compare before and after.
