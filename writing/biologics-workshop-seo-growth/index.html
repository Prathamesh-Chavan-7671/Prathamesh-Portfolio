What Two Years of SEO Did for Biologics Workshop's Traffic

Since October 2024 I've handled the SEO and website optimisation for Biologics Workshop. Here's what the visitor numbers actually show for it.

By Prathamesh Chavan16 November 20264 min read

  • SEO
  • Digital growth
  • Content strategy
  • Analytics

Where it started

Since October 2024 I've worked on the SEO and website optimisation for Biologics Workshop, a platform for the biopharmaceutical and life sciences field. The brief was easy to state and slow to execute: make the site easier to find, and worth staying on once someone gets there.

What the work actually was

Most of it was unglamorous and repeated often. Rewriting page content to match what people were actually searching for. Keyword research and checking it against real search intent, not guesses. Fixing meta titles, descriptions and headings. Working through technical SEO and indexing issues. Restructuring internal links so related pages actually connect. And checking the analytics every month to see what moved and what didn't.

None of it is a single fix. It's the same handful of things, done again on the next page, the next month.

The numbers

Unique visitors, by period:

PeriodUnique visitors
October–December 202410,493
Full year 202530,955
January–October 202636,335

Three things stand out in that data. January to October 2026 brought 45.7% more unique visitors than the same ten months of 2025. October 2026 alone had 4,843 visitors against 1,232 in October 2024 — roughly four times as many, two years apart. And by October, 2026 had already passed the whole of 2025's visitor count, by about 17.4%.

What I learned

No single change moved that number. It's the accumulation of a lot of small, repeated fixes over many months, which is a less satisfying story than a single growth hack but a more honest one.

The data is what tells you where to look next, not instinct. Traffic broken down by page and by search query is what actually shows whether a change helped or did nothing.

And it doesn't finish. Search engines keep changing how they rank things, competitors keep publishing, and the list of pages worth revisiting never really empties out.

It's ongoing work for me, and the next stretch is more of the same: keep an eye on what's working, fix what isn't, and look for the next page worth improving.
